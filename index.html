02

The argument

The argument follows the three steps you set out. Organizational Learning Theory is at the centre. The other two perspectives enter only where they explain something the centre does not.

Safety is a condition for learning

An organization learns when people admit errors, question how things are done and pass on what they know. Each of these carries a personal risk: of looking ignorant, of being blamed. Psychological safety lowers that risk. Edmondson built the construct to explain learning behaviour, which is why Organizational Learning Theory is its natural home.

Sharing turns safety into behaviour

A person can feel safe and still keep what they know to themselves. Safety lowers the risk; sharing is the act. Innovation needs that act. New ideas are built from knowledge that others pass on, and a componential view of innovation explains how shared knowledge and a supportive setting feed new ideas.

Leadership turns safety into sharing

Where colleagues rarely meet, sharing does not happen by itself. A leader brings people together, opens channels, asks for views and shows that sharing is expected. That is the moderation: the same level of safety leads to more sharing under a more transformational leader.

Why dispersion matters to the argument

On a single campus, colleagues meet every day and knowledge passes between them without anyone arranging it. At Al-Quds Open University, members of one work team can sit in different branches and cities. So feeling safe is less likely to be enough. The everyday chances to share are fewer, and someone has to create them.

This is how the study answers your question of whether dispersion changes the way safety becomes sharing. The leader is the condition that dispersion makes necessary. No extra variable is needed for the argument. Section 06 shows how dispersion is also checked in the data.

Each role of leadership has its own reason

As an antecedent of safety (Model B): Social Exchange Theory. A leader who supports, listens and does not punish errors is repaid with trust, and employees feel safer to speak.

As a moderator of the path from safety to sharing: Organizational Learning Theory. Where informal contact is scarce, the leader supplies the structure through which a safe employee actually shares.

The two roles do not compete. One says leadership builds the condition. The other says leadership turns the condition into action.

The contribution, in one line: the study tests whether, in a dispersed organization, leadership is the condition under which psychological safety becomes knowledge sharing, and through it innovative behaviour.

One outcome: innovative work behaviour

Version 1 had two outcomes. This version keeps one. Four reasons.

You preferred a focused article. A study with fewer variables and a clear question is easier to defend in review.

The question is about behaviour. Innovative work behaviour is a behaviour. Learning commitment is an attitude.

It removes the weakest measure. The learning-commitment items come second-hand from a 1998 paper and were validated on 209 factory workers in Taiwan.

The finding from version 1 is kept, in a smaller role. The source study called its outcome innovation commitment, but its items measure learning. This study measures innovation directly, with Janssen's validated scale in Al-Qarni's Arabic version. In the method chapter this becomes a measurement correction, not a second outcome.

Psychological safety: the work team, as an individual perception

This is Edmondson's own referent, so the scale is used exactly as published. Respondents read one line first: "Your work team means the colleagues you work with most closely in your daily work, whether they are in your branch or in other branches." The construct is each employee's own perception. It is not aggregated into a team or organizational climate.

Knowledge sharing: behaviour, not intention

Version 1 used Bock et al.'s items, which measure intention ("I will share"). This version uses knowledge donating: what the employee does ("I share the information I have with colleagues"). The scale's companion, knowledge collecting, is not used: its items describe what colleagues do when the respondent asks, not the respondent's own behaviour. Two adaptations, both stated in the method chapter: "department" becomes "work team", so that safety and sharing refer to the same people; and the security qualifier in the Arabic source, which was written for a police force, is removed.

Transformational leadership: the full inventory, about the immediate supervisor

Version 1 used five items, worded about top management, that covered about one of Podsakoff's six dimensions. The inventory covers all six: articulating a vision (5 items), providing an appropriate model (3), fostering acceptance of group goals (4), high performance expectations (3), individualized support (4) and intellectual stimulation (3), 22 in all. This is the authors' own 1996 refinement of their 1990 scale, and it is the version that exists in Arabic. The original study asked about the immediate supervisor. In a dispersed university, that is also the leader an employee actually deals with.

Arabic versions

All four scales are used in a published Arabic version. Psychological safety from Davis et al. (2023), the leadership inventory from Alharbi (2018), knowledge donating from Bahar (2018), and innovative work behaviour from Al-Qarni (2022). Each was produced with documented back-translation, and each source is named in the method chapter. Nothing here is translated by me. A pilot still runs before fieldwork, and it pays particular attention to the three reverse-scored safety items: the one earlier Arabic version of that scale returned alphas of .56 and .40.

Model A and Model B

As you asked, both specifications are estimated and compared before one is chosen.

Model A · for comparison

The original moderated mediation

Safety leads to sharing, and sharing to innovative behaviour. Leadership moderates the path from safety to sharing, and has a direct path to sharing, because a main effect must be in the model whenever its interaction is. Safety also has a direct path to the outcome, so the type of mediation can be tested.

Model B · main model

The extended leadership model

Model A plus one path: leadership leads to safety. Leadership then plays two roles, each with its own theory (section 02).

How the two are compared: explained variance in each construct, out-of-sample prediction with PLSpredict, and whether the added path holds. Collinearity is checked because leadership enters the model twice.

How dispersion enters the data

In the definition of the team, which includes colleagues in other branches.

In the sharing scale, which has three items about sharing inside one's own team and three about sharing outside it. Sharing across unit lines is what dispersion makes hard, and the two halves can be compared.

In one background question: do your closest colleagues work in your branch, in other branches, or both? If both groups are large enough, the path from safety to sharing is compared between them. This is reported as an exploratory check, not a hypothesis, to keep the model lean.

As a control: where the employee works, in five categories entered as four dummy variables: northern, central and southern West Bank, Gaza, and the central administration. The official record shows 878 staff in the 18 branches and 259 in 27 central units, deanships and faculties. Central staff sit at a head office and do not face what dispersion does to the branches, so they are their own category rather than being folded into a region.

Method

Common method variance cannot create an interaction effect; it can only make a real one smaller (Siemsen, Roth & Oliveira, 2010). This finding is debated, so it is one argument among several. The others: separate sections, different response formats, an anonymous matching code between waves, and a full-collinearity check of the variance inflation factors.

How these numbers were produced

The hypothesis that decides this study is the moderation, so the power analysis is for a single coefficient, not for the model as a whole: the coefficient of the interaction in the regression of knowledge sharing on its eight predictors. That fixes the degrees of freedom.

Numerator df
1, one coefficient tested
Denominator df
N − k − 1, with k = 8 predictors
Significance
0.05
Target power
0.80

This is why adding the fifth workplace category changed nothing: it raises k by one, which moves the denominator degrees of freedom by one, not the sample needed.

The effect size is Cohen's f squared, the share of unexplained variance the interaction accounts for:

f² = (R²_full − R²_reduced) / (1 − R²_full)

Cohen's conventions are 0.02 small, 0.15 medium, 0.35 large. Interactions in field data sit at or below the small end, so 0.02 is the figure the design is built on, and the larger effect in the Egyptian study is reported only as a comparison.

Power comes from the non-central F distribution, with non-centrality λ:

λ = f² × N power = 1 − F_cdf( F_crit ; df1, df2, λ ) F_crit = F₀.₉₅(df1, df2)

At N = 394 power is 0.800 and at N = 396 it is 0.802, so 395 is the smallest sample that reaches the convention. The other rows follow the same way: f squared 0.164 gives 51, and 0.05 gives 160.

A second check, specific to PLS-SEM. Regression power analysis is an approximation for a PLS model, so the inverse square root method of Kock and Hadaya (2018) is reported beside it. For 5% significance and 80% power, with p the smallest path expected to be significant:

N > ( 2.486 / |p| )² p = 0.15 → N > (2.486 / 0.15)² = 274.7 → N = 275

The two answers differ, and both are reported. The inverse square root method asks what the smallest main path needs; the regression analysis asks what the interaction needs. The interaction is the harder test and the larger number, so 395 governs.

The response-rate rows are the same calculation applied to a census of the frame, and the threshold is the rate that delivers 395:

n = 1,126 × response rate 395 / 1,126 = 35.1% matched pairs: 1,126 × 0.42 × 0.70 = 331

Software. Computed with the non-central F distribution. The same inputs in G*Power, under Linear multiple regression: fixed model, R² increase, with 1 tested predictor and 8 total, return the same figures.

What is not claimed. These are a priori calculations on assumed effect sizes. They say what the design can detect, not what it will find. The observed effect sizes and the achieved power are reported once the data are in.
