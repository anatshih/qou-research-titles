01

The title and the model

Proposed title

The Effect of Psychological Safety on Learning Commitment and Innovative Work Behaviour among Employees at Al-Quds Open University: The Mediating Role of Knowledge Sharing and the Moderating Role of Transformational Leadership

Psychological safety and knowledge sharing are both constructs from inside organizational culture, each with its own validated measure. Transformational leadership enters as the moderator. There are two outcomes, and section 02 explains why.

Select any box to see its measure. An arrow into the outcomes panel runs to each of the two outcomes. Arrows marked direct are direct effects, tested next to the path through sharing. The dashed grey arrow is the moderation of the path from safety to sharing. The dashed amber arrow is the one path added to the published model; section 07 explains it.

02

Why there are two outcomes

The five items the published model uses for its outcome do not measure what the model calls them. All five are about training and learning opportunities. Section 06 sets out the evidence, with every original scale paper in hand.

So the first outcome is named for what its items measure, and a second outcome is added. That turns a measurement problem into the question the study answers.

Outcome one

Learning commitment

The published five items, named for what they measure. The original paper is titled for learning commitment and holds no innovation variable at all.

This strengthens the model. Edmondson's 1999 paper, which supplies the predictor, is titled Psychological Safety and Learning Behavior in Work Teams. Her construct predicts learning, so the chain from safety, through sharing, to commitment to learning holds end to end. The published model reached for innovation and broke its alignment with its own anchor theory.

Cost: nothing. The five items are unchanged.

Outcome two

Innovative work behaviour

Nine items from Janssen (2000), whose author validated them for self-report and reported reliability of 0.95. An Arabic standardisation already exists, by Al-Qarni (2022), on five-point frequency anchors at reliability 0.89, and it has since been applied at 0.94.

With both outcomes the study answers a question the literature has not, because it has conflated the two: does psychological safety, through knowledge sharing, build commitment to learning, innovative behaviour, or both, and with what relative strength?

Cost: nine items and a limited expert review for fit to higher education. The instrument runs to 30 items in total, all on a five-point scale.

Commitment to learning, commitment to innovation and innovative work behaviour are three different constructs, and none is treated as interchangeable with another. Whether the two outcomes separate empirically is itself tested, and either answer is reportable.

A form of its own

It is the first blended-learning university in the Arab world, and it states its own vision as leadership in blended higher education. It joins printed course material, classroom meetings, face-to-face exams, self-study and e-learning in one system. Its staff work inside a teaching model that regular universities do not have, so findings taken from regular universities cannot simply be carried over.

Spread is a fact, not a phrase

It was set up to work around the closure of the Palestinian territories, by taking teaching to where students already are. That purpose produced 18 branches in different cities, and the staff of one faculty are spread over them. This is why sharing knowledge here is a matter of structure and not of goodwill, and it is the reason the mediator is worth testing again.

Scale is enough

With 33,548 students across 18 branches, the staff body is large enough for the sample this model needs, and spread widely enough for differences between regions to mean something. The survey goes to all 18 branches, not to one branch standing for the rest.

05

The gap this paper fills

The authors end their paper by asking for the model to be tested in other kinds of institution and other countries, and they state that their results may not hold for public universities. Al-Quds Open University meets both points.

It adds a third. It is an open and blended university, and its staff work in branches in different cities. Sharing knowledge between people who never meet on one campus is a matter of structure, not of goodwill. So the mediator itself stands under a different kind of pressure than it did in the source study, and that is a theoretical reason to retest it, not only a new population.

And a fourth, which came out of reading the original scale papers: three of the four measures in the published model carry names their items do not support. A replication that corrects the naming is a contribution to the measurement literature as well as a test of the model. Section 06 is the evidence.

How the outcome scale travelled

Its reliability is the highest of the five measures, 0.936. That tells us the items measure one thing tightly, not that they measure the thing in the name. Reliability is not validity.

What I propose to do about it

Keep the published model as the base, so the replication stays clean, and name each construct for what its items support. Report this audit in the method chapter with the originals cited, and discuss it as a finding about the literature. A replication that finds a measurement problem in the original is a contribution, not a flaw.

07

Where leadership sits, and the one addition proposed

Most of the recent work puts transformational leadership before psychological safety rather than treating it as a moderator. The published model can answer that, and the answer suggests an extension worth making. None of the five studies below carries an interaction term, so none of them tests moderation at all.

Five studies that order the variables differently. The first is the closest to this setting.
StudyDesignWhat it reports

The answer the published model already gives

Transformational leadership is in that model as a direct predictor as well as a moderator. Its effect sizes are 0.088 for the main effect and 0.164 for the interaction. The interaction is the larger of the two. So moderation is not a substitute for the antecedent logic, and the published evidence says the interaction carries more weight.

The addition: leadership to psychological safety

The one path the published model leaves out is leadership to safety, which is exactly what those studies test. The strongest number for it comes from the study closest to this setting: Salifu and colleagues report 0.753 on higher education staff, with an f squared of 1.306, while leadership’s direct effect on the outcome in the same model is only 0.156. Adding the path costs nothing: the same data, the same items, no extra question on the form.

With it, the paper tests three things together that no study in this stream has tested together. Whether leadership creates safety. Whether it amplifies the effect of safety on sharing. And whether it reaches sharing directly, independent of safety. The second and third are not in competition: one says leadership builds the condition, the other says leadership makes the condition pay off.

Specifying a variable as both an antecedent and a moderator needs care in PLS-SEM. Variance inflation factors will be reported, the interaction term built by the two-stage approach, and both roles justified in the method chapter. All VIFs in the source study were below 3, so collinearity was not a problem there.

On Ullah and colleagues and on Febrianto and colleagues: judge the journals before leaning on them. They are cited as evidence that the rival ordering is common. The theoretical argument rests on Salifu and colleagues in Educational Research and Evaluation, on the integrative review, and on Edmondson's own framework.

Fieldwork

The plan as it stands. Hypotheses follow once the title is settled.
Population990 staff across all 18 branches: 320 academic, 670 administrative
Item referentThe whole university, matching the source study
ControlsRegion — the 18 branches grouped into northern, central and southern West Bank, and Gaza — and length of service. Eighteen branches would enter as 17 dummy variables and take the predictors on knowledge sharing from five to twenty, destroying the power the sample is designed for. Four regions enter as three dummies, keep the dispersion argument, and add a West Bank against Gaza comparison
DistributionThe whole population of 990, with university approval. Not a drawn sample: at the local response rate, distributing only to 277 would yield about 89
Required sample277. Two independent routes converge on it: Cochran's formula with a finite population correction at a 5% margin and 95% confidence, and an a priori power analysis. At 277 the achieved power is 0.98 for an effect of f squared 0.088, the size of the leadership main effect in the source study, and 0.83 for a conservative 0.05
Expected yieldA 2026 study at this university drew 261 responses from a population of 804, a ratio of 32.5%. At that rate a census here returns about 322, clearing 277 with margin
Instrument30 items, all on a five-point scale: 7 psychological safety, 4 knowledge sharing, 5 learning commitment, 5 transformational leadership, 9 innovative work behaviour. The safety scale carries all seven of Edmondson’s original items, three of them reverse scored; the behaviour scale uses five-point frequency anchors, following the Arabic standardisation
AnalysisPLS-SEM in SmartPLS, bootstrap resampling, two-stage interaction terms, and HTMT between the two outcomes
Group comparisonStaff category is asked as one question. A proportional yield gives about 90 academic respondents, below the 152 needed to power a full multi-group comparison, so what is reported is a measurement invariance test between the two groups. The full comparison is named in the recommendations
Order of workApproval and the Arabic instrument, then a pilot on a small group, then the survey across all branches, then the model, then writing
